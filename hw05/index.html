Welford's Algorithm & Sampling Statistics

Exercise 1 - Welford's Recurrence Formulas

Following the same approach as Knuth's recurrence for the mean, we derive Welford's online algorithm for the sum of squares and variance.

Knuth's Recurrence for the Mean

Given a new observation \(x_n\), the mean can be updated incrementally: \[M_n = M_{n-1} + \frac{x_n - M_{n-1}}{n}\]

Derivation of Welford's SS Recurrence

Define \(SS_n = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - M_n)^2\). We want to express \(SS_n\) in terms of \(SS_{n-1}\) and the new observation \(x_n\).

Starting from the definition and expanding: \[SS_n = SS_{n-1} + (x_n - M_{n-1})(x_n - M_n)\]

This elegant result uses both the old mean \(M_{n-1}\) and the new mean \(M_n\), making it numerically stable, unlike the naive formula \(\sum x_i^2 - n\bar{x}^2\) which suffers from catastrophic cancellation for large values.

Variance

The sample variance (with Bessel's correction) is: \[s^2_n = \frac{SS_n}{n-1}\]

Bessel's correction (\(n-1\) instead of \(n\)) makes \(s^2_n\) an unbiased estimator of the population variance \(\sigma^2\).

Implementation

The core logic requires only 3 lines:

delta  = x - mean_prev          // deviation from old mean
mean   = mean_prev + delta / n   // Knuth's update
SS     = SS_prev + delta * (x - mean)  // Welford's update (uses new mean)

Results

Distribution of Sampling Means

By the Central Limit Theorem, the sampling means \(\bar{X}\) are approximately normally distributed with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2/n\), regardless of the original distribution.

Variance of Sampling Means vs n

As \(n\) increases, \(\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \sigma^2/n \to 0\), the sampling means concentrate around \(\mu\) (Law of Large Numbers).

Discussion

  • Average of sampling means ≈ μ: the mean of the sampling means converges to the theoretical mean regardless of \(n\), the sampling mean is an unbiased estimator of \(\mu\).
  • Average of sampling variances ≈ σ²: thanks to Bessel's correction (\(n-1\) denominator), the sampling variance is an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma^2\). Without it, the estimator would systematically underestimate \(\sigma^2\).
  • Increasing n reduces variability: as \(n\) grows, \(\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \sigma^2/n \to 0\), each hacker's sampling mean becomes more reliable. This is the practical implication of the Law of Large Numbers.
  • Usefulness in inferential statistics: these results justify using sample statistics to estimate population parameters. With sufficient \(n\), a single hacker's sampling mean is a reliable estimate of \(\mu\), and the distribution of sampling means allows confidence interval construction.

Conclusion

Welford's algorithm provides a numerically stable, single-pass method for computing mean and variance, essential for streaming data where storing all values is impractical. The simulation confirms that sampling means and variances are unbiased estimators of their population counterparts, and that increasing \(n\) reduces the variability of sampling means as predicted by \(\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \sigma^2/n\).
