ECC Encryption & Caesar Cipher

Executive Summary

This homework compares two cryptographic scheme ElGamal encryption on the toy elliptic curve from HW01, and the classical Caesar cipher, analyzing their encryption and decryption procedures, key spaces, and statistical properties. It also implements attacks against both schemes, exploiting their respective weaknesses.

ECC Encryption (ElGamal on Elliptic Curve)

ElGamal encryption on an elliptic curve works as follows:

  • Setup: curve \(y^2 \equiv x^3 + 7 \pmod{17}\), base point \(G\), private key \(k\), public key \(Q = kG\).
  • Message embedding: each character is mapped to a point on the curve via a lookup table.
  • Encryption: for each character \(M\), choose random \(r\) and compute \(C_1 = rG\), \(C_2 = M + rQ\). Ciphertext is \((C_1, C_2)\).
  • Decryption: compute \(rQ = k \cdot C_1\), then \(M = C_2 - rQ\).

Security relies on the ECDLP, knowing \(Q = kG\) and \(C_1 = rG\) does not reveal \(k\) or \(r\) without solving the discrete logarithm.

Caesar Cipher

The Caesar cipher shifts each letter by a fixed amount \(s\): \(E(c) = (c + s) \bmod 26\), \(D(c) = (c - s) \bmod 26\). Non-letter characters are left unchanged.

The key space is only 26, exhaustive search is trivial. Moreover, Caesar preserves letter frequencies, making it vulnerable to frequency analysis.

Statistical Comparison

Caesar cipher preserves letter frequency distribution, the index of coincidence remains high (~0.065 for English). ECC operates on curve points rather than letters, so standard letter statistics do not apply.

Discussion

  • Key space: Caesar has 26 possible keys, trivially exhaustible. The toy ECC scheme has at most 18 keys (group order), also small, but for fundamentally different reasons. Real secp256k1 has \(2^{256}\) possible keys, computationally infeasible to brute force.
  • Statistical vulnerability: Caesar preserves letter frequencies, frequency analysis recovers the key from ~50 characters. ECC produces ciphertexts that are curve points, no letter frequency structure to exploit.
  • Attack comparison: the Caesar attack exploits statistical structure in the plaintext language. The ECC attack exploits the restricted key space, it is a mathematical attack, not a statistical one. On real secp256k1 the mathematical attack is infeasible because the key space is astronomically large.
  • Why the toy scheme is weak: the restriction to a small prime field (mod 17) limits the group order to at most 18, making both brute force and baby-step giant-step trivial. The weakness comes entirely from the small key space, not from the mathematical structure of ECC itself.

Conclusion

The Caesar cipher and ECC encryption represent opposite ends of the cryptographic spectrum. Caesar is simple, deterministic, and completely broken by frequency analysis. ECC is mathematically sophisticated, semantically secure (with proper randomness in \(r\)), and resistant to statistical attacks. The toy scheme shares the mathematical correctness of real ECC but inherits the weakness of a small key space, demonstrating that security in ECC comes not from the algorithm itself but from the size of the underlying field.
