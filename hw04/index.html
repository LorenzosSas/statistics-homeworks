Stochastic Penetration Model

Executive Summary

This project simulates a stochastic penetration process modeled by independent and identically distributed increments \(X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n\), studying the behavior of the total score \(S_n\), average score \(S_n/n\), and normalized score \(S_n/\sqrt{n}\) under two models: Bernoulli and Rademacher.

The simulation investigates the Law of Large Numbers (convergence of \(S_n/n\)) and the Central Limit Theorem (convergence of \(S_n/\sqrt{n}\) to a normal distribution).

Model Description

Consider \(n\) systems and \(m\) attackers. Each attacker performs \(n\) independent penetration attempts, with score \(X_i\) at step \(i\):

  • Bernoulli model: \(X_i \in \{0, 1\}\) with \(P(X_i = 1) = p\). Models a binary success/failure penetration attempt. \(E[X_i] = p\), \(\text{Var}[X_i] = p(1-p)\).
  • Rademacher model: \(X_i \in \{-1, +1\}\) with \(P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = \frac{1}{2}\). Models a symmetric, zero-mean process. \(E[X_i] = 0\), \(\text{Var}[X_i] = 1\).

Three quantities are studied for increasing \(n\):

  • \(S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\), total score (grows with \(n\))
  • \(S_n/n\), average score (converges to \(E[X_i]\) by LLN)
  • \(S_n/\sqrt{n}\), normalized score (converges to \(N(0, \sigma^2)\) by CLT)

Theoretical Background

  • Law of Large Numbers: \(S_n/n \xrightarrow{P} E[X_i]\) as \(n \to \infty\). The average score converges in probability to the theoretical mean. Variance of \(S_n/n\) is \(\text{Var}[X_i]/n \to 0\).
  • Central Limit Theorem: \(\frac{S_n - nE[X_i]}{\sqrt{n\,\text{Var}[X_i]}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)\) as \(n \to \infty\). In the Rademacher case \(E[X_i] = 0\) and \(\text{Var}[X_i] = 1\), so \(S_n/\sqrt{n} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1)\).
  • Variance behavior: \(\text{Var}[S_n] = n\,\text{Var}[X_i]\) grows linearly; \(\text{Var}[S_n/n] = \text{Var}[X_i]/n\) shrinks to 0; \(\text{Var}[S_n/\sqrt{n}] = \text{Var}[X_i]\) stays constant.

Discussion

  • Total score \(S_n\): variance grows linearly with \(n\), trajectories spread out over time. In the Bernoulli case, \(S_n\) drifts upward (mean = \(np\)); in the Rademacher case it oscillates around 0.
  • Average score \(S_n/n\): converges to \(E[X_i]\) as \(n\) increases (Law of Large Numbers). Variance shrinks to 0, trajectories concentrate around the theoretical mean.
  • Normalized score \(S_n/\sqrt{n}\): variance stabilizes at \(\text{Var}[X_i]\). In the Rademacher case, the distribution converges to \(N(0,1)\) (Central Limit Theorem).
  • System size and number of attackers: increasing \(n\) makes the LLN and CLT convergence more visible. Increasing \(m\) gives a better empirical distribution of the final scores.

Conclusion

The simulation confirms the theoretical predictions: \(S_n/n\) converges to the theoretical mean as \(n\) grows (LLN), while \(S_n/\sqrt{n}\) converges in distribution to a normal with variance equal to \(\text{Var}[X_i]\) (CLT). The Rademacher case provides the cleanest demonstration of the CLT due to its zero mean and unit variance. The Bernoulli case shows how a non-symmetric distribution still converges to normality under the CLT.
